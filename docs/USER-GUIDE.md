# Krīḍā WLog

A strength log that keeps its data on your phone. No account, no network, no
permissions.

This covers what the interface does not explain by itself. Everything else is
meant to be found by using it.

---

## Start here

**More → Equipment.** Add the weights you actually own — each dumbbell, each
plate, the increments on your cable stacks. Do it once.

This is what turns logging into tapping. When you open a set, the weights you
own appear as chips in their own units, so a 32.5 kg stack and a 70 lb stack
sit side by side and neither is converted into the other. Skip this step and
you will type every number by hand for months.

---

## Getting around

**Routines** — your plans, and where a session starts.
**Calendar** — planned days against logged ones, with a body map beneath.
**Home** — the middle tab, where the app opens.
**Logbook** — everything you have done.
**More** — equipment, library, schedules, export, restore, settings.

---

## Logging

Start a routine and every set arrives pre-filled from the last time you did
that exercise **in that routine**. Bench on Monday does not borrow from bench
on Friday.

Pre-filled numbers are shown faintly. They have not happened yet.

- Numbers right → tap **Log**.
- Numbers wrong → tap the row, change them, log.
- Logged by mistake → tap the green tick to undo.

Anything still faint when you finish is discarded.

### Units

The unit belongs to the **set**, not the exercise, because the same movement
gets done on a machine marked in pounds one day and kilograms the next. Tap
the unit beside the weight to switch it.

What you typed is what you see, always. Kilograms are computed once from it
for the totals, and nothing is ever converted back for display.

## Routines

Each exercise holds how many sets, what to aim for, and how long to rest.
Leave the second box of a target empty for a single figure rather than a
range.

**Different values per set** turns that into a table, one row per set.
Anything left blank falls back to the target above, so a drop set means
filling in one row rather than four.

What you actually lifted last time still beats both. A target is an
intention; history is evidence.

## Things that are not obvious

**Sessions can be entered late.** Any routine's menu, or the button on the
Logbook. Date required, times optional. Filled in from what came *before*
that date, not from your most recent session.

**Swipe a routine** — one way archives it, the other deletes it. Archiving is
reversible and keeps the plan; deleting keeps only the sessions logged from
it.

**Unilateral exercises rate each side separately.** Same load, harder on one
side, is the signal worth watching.

**The body map counts sets against primary muscles only.** Secondary muscles
appear so much more often that including them would leave the same regions
permanently hottest. Grey means nothing logged, not lightly logged.

**Nothing chases you.** Miss a scheduled day and nothing marks it. The
schedule says what was planned, the logbook says what happened, and they are
allowed to disagree.

## Getting your data out

**More → Export** writes a spreadsheet of every set, a full backup, or one
file per session. Filters narrow the spreadsheet and the session files. The
backup is never narrowed — a partial backup would restore without complaint
and leave gaps with nothing to announce them.

**Logbook → select** picks particular sessions to export, together or
separately.

**More → Restore** brings a backup back, whole or in part. It replaces what
is there rather than merging, so export first if today matters.

Set an export folder under More, or files land somewhere Android hides from
file managers.

---

## Deliberately absent

- **Supersets.** Log them as separate exercises.
- **Warm-up flags.** Every logged set counts toward volume.
- **Reminders.** The schedule shows what is due; nothing notifies you. That
  is what keeps the app free of any Android permission.

---

## Track sessions

**Home → Track session.** A stopwatch for intervals, drawn black because it
is read outdoors at arm's length.

**400 m** and **200 m** start the clock and record a rep per tap. The phone
times and nothing else — the distance is whichever button you press.

Tones sound at three, two and one second of recovery remaining, then a longer
one as the next rep starts. The speaker icon mutes them.
